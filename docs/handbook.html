Section 1

The 60-second version

If you read nothing else, read this.

In one sentence

NoPS is a web app where a business person uploads a spreadsheet of their sales/operations data and instantly gets a professional analytics dashboard, forecasts, automatic alerts, plain-English answers to questions, and a downloadable PDF report — without hiring a data analyst.

The five things that matter

  1. It’s a real, working product, not a prototype. You can run it today with one command and log in with a demo account. It has real users’ concerns baked in: accounts, teams, billing tiers, security.
  2. Its whole selling point is trust. Every number it shows is calculated by plain, readable code that gives the same answer every time — never guessed by an AI. That’s the meaning of the word “deterministic” you’ll see everywhere. It’s aimed at people who can’t afford a chatbot to make up a figure (finance, regulated industries).
  3. AI is used carefully and optionally. The only place AI appears is to understand your typed question (“which products are declining?”) and translate it into an instruction. The actual math is still done by the trustworthy code. And your data rows are never sent to any AI — only the question text and column names.
  4. It has been renamed once. It started life as “DecisionIQ” and was renamed to “NoPS” in August 2026. Same product, newer name. You’ll still find “DecisionIQ” in older commit history.
  5. There’s a much bigger vision written down but not yet built: a “generative design system” that would auto-design a uniquely styled dashboard for each business (a restaurant vs. a fintech get different looks), while keeping the number-crunching identical and trustworthy. Today the dashboard adapts its content per industry, but not yet its visual design.

Where it is right now

A feature-complete MVP (minimum viable product) with an unusually strong engineering foundation for its age: multi-tenant accounts, role-based permissions, a tested deterministic analytics engine, forecasting with scenarios, anomaly and driver detection, scheduled reports, alert rules, data-source connectors, billing tiers, and automated security scanning. The next frontier is the generative design vision (Section 14) and turning billing from a surface into real Stripe charges.

Section 2

What NoPS is, in plain English

Imagine you run a business — a shop, a pharmacy, a small software company. You have a spreadsheet of every sale. It has the answers you need buried in it, but pulling them out means either learning to build charts yourself or paying an analyst.

NoPS is the analyst. You drag your spreadsheet onto the page, and within moments it:

  • figures out what each column means (which one is revenue, which is the date, which is the customer, the region, the product…) — on its own;
  • checks the data for problems (missing values, duplicates, typos, outliers) and offers to clean them — without ever changing your original file;
  • builds a dashboard: the key numbers (revenue, profit, margin, orders, customers), how they changed vs. the previous period, trend lines, and rankings (top products, top customers, best regions);
  • lets you ask questions in normal English and answers them;
  • predicts where things are heading (forecasts);
  • flags anything alarming automatically (a revenue drop, a looming stockout);
  • and exports a clean PDF report you could hand to a board or a bank.
The promise on the tin

“Upload business data → get automated dashboards, forecasts, alerts, a natural-language query interface, and board-ready PDF reports. Multi-tenant, role-based, works out of the box with sample retail data.” — that is the literal opening of the project’s README, and it’s accurate.

What makes it different from “just use Excel” or “just ask ChatGPT”

ApproachThe catch NoPS avoids
Excel / Google SheetsYou still have to build every chart, formula and pivot yourself, and redo it each month.
Ask an AI chatbot to analyse the fileThe AI can silently invent a number that looks plausible but is wrong — unacceptable for money or compliance. NoPS never lets AI produce a figure.
Hire a BI tool (Tableau, Power BI)Powerful but heavy: you configure everything, it assumes you already know what you want. NoPS auto-generates the first useful view from the raw file.

So the identity is: the speed and automation of an AI tool, with the trustworthiness of a spreadsheet formula you can audit line by line.

Section 3

The one big idea: “deterministic”

This word appears everywhere in the codebase. It is the product’s entire personality. Here’s what it means and why it was chosen.

The plain meaning

Deterministic = same input always produces the same output, forever, and you can read exactly how. The opposite is an AI language model, which can give slightly different (or wrong) answers to the same question and can’t show its arithmetic.

The line the whole product rests on

“Every number is deterministic and reproducible. Schema detection, data profiling, forecasting, KPIs, and recommendations are all real code you can read, unit test, audit, and run offline — no model produces a figure. Given a question’s intent, the same dataset yields the same answer a million times.”

Why this is a genuinely smart bet

For the customer

  • Auditable — every figure has a code path you can step through. Regulator- and finance-friendly.
  • Reproducible — the same report next quarter is computed the same way.
  • Private — your data rows never leave your servers.
  • Fast — sub-100ms answers; no round-trip to a big AI model.

For the business (economics)

  • Free at rest — the engine has no per-question AI bill, no rate limits, no vendor invoice.
  • The only part that could ever cost per-use is the optional question-understanding AI, and it’s off unless a key is set.
  • That means margins don’t erode as usage grows — the classic problem with AI-wrapper products.

So where is the AI, exactly?

In precisely one place: the natural-language chat box. When you type “top 10 customers,” the system needs to turn that English into a structured instruction. If an OPENAI_API_KEY is configured, it asks GPT to do that translation. If no key is set, a rule-based parser (regex and keywords) does it instead — fully offline. Either way, the actual answer is computed by the deterministic engine, and the AI only ever sees your question text and your column names — never your data.

The mental model to keep

AI is the translator at the front desk. The deterministic engine is the accountant in the back room. The translator never touches the ledger.

Section 5

Who it’s for & how it makes money

The intended customer

Small-to-mid businesses and teams that have data but no dedicated analyst, and — crucially — that care whether the numbers are right and defensible. The deterministic positioning specifically targets finance, regulated industries, and anyone who has to justify a figure to a board, an auditor, or a regulator. The seeded demo is a retail shop, but the app ships with industry packs that reshape the dashboard per business type.

Industry packs (how one app fits many businesses)

An “industry pack” is data, not new code. Each pack declares the extra vocabulary to recognise, which KPIs matter and how to compute them, and how to label the dashboard. Adding a new industry means adding one entry. Packs shipped today:

Section 8

How it’s built — the architecture

For the technically curious. Even if you’re not, the shape here is simple and worth seeing once.

The project is a monorepo — one repository holding two applications that share tooling:

apps/
  api/                    ← the "back end": Express + TypeScript REST API
    prisma/               schema, migrations, seed, sample-data generator
    src/
      engine/             THE DETERMINISTIC CORE — pure, testable functions
      auth/               JWT, requireAuth, requireRole
      modules/            one router per domain (uploads, datasets, analytics, ai, …)
      ai/                 optional GPT question-understanding (translate-only)
      billing/            plan definitions & limit checks
      scheduler.ts        background jobs: scheduled reports + alert rules
  web/                    ← the "front end": React + Vite + TypeScript + Tailwind
    src/{components,pages,lib}
docs/                     architecture + roadmap docs (and this handbook)
docker-compose.yml        one-command startup (Postgres + API + web)

The back end (apps/api)

A REST API in Node/Express/TypeScript. Its job: handle accounts and permissions, receive uploads, store data, and — above all — run the engine. Data lives in PostgreSQL, accessed through Prisma (a type-safe database toolkit). Uploaded rows are stored as JSON on the dataset record rather than in a per-cell table — a deliberate, simple choice that’s correct up to ~100k-row files.

The front end (apps/web)

A single-page React app built with Vite. Tailwind for styling, Recharts for charts, TanStack Query for data-fetching, React Router for pages, framer-motion for the animation polish. Dark-mode-first. In production, the API even serves the built web files, so the whole product runs on one port.

The request flow, end to end

Upload a file
   → parse (CSV/XLSX → rows)
   → profile (stats + data-quality issues)
   → detect schema (which column means what)
   → store as a Dataset
Open the dashboard
   → engine computes KPIs, trends, rankings for the detected industry pack
   → forecast / insights / anomalies / drivers derived on demand
   → React renders it
Ask a question
   → (optional GPT) translate English → structured intent
   → validate intent against a closed vocabulary
   → deterministic analytics computes the answer
   → render as table / chart / metric
The design philosophy, stated

“One deterministic implementation, no provider abstraction. Predictability is the product.” The team intentionally avoided speculative abstractions and kept the codebase lean.

Section 9

The engine — the crown jewels

Everything valuable about NoPS lives in apps/api/src/engine/: ~2,400 lines of pure TypeScript with no I/O, no randomness, no external calls. Each file is one responsibility, and every bug ever fixed here got a permanent test (“regression assert”) so it can’t come back.

FileWhat it does
parse.tsTurns a raw CSV/XLSX file into typed rows. Handles buffer validation, column inference, XLSX normalisation.
profile.tsProfiles each column and finds data-quality issues (missing values, outliers, duplicates, type mismatches).
schema.tsThe “brain” that maps columns to business meaning (revenue, date, region, customer…). Also does row cleaning.
analytics.tsThe controlled query layer: KPI computation, time series, group-by aggregations, multi-value filters. Everything the dashboard and chat ultimately call.
intent.tsNatural-language query → structured intent → answer. The closed-vocabulary safety net that keeps NL queries honest.
forecast.tsForecasting: seasonal model selection, trend, best/base/worst scenarios, and a residual-based 95% confidence band that widens with horizon.
anomaly.tsDeterministic anomaly detection — flags unusual data points.
drivers.tsDriver analysis — explains what moved a metric (which product/region), with an “other” remainder so the shown movers reconcile.
correlate.tsFinds correlations between metrics.
segment.tsValue-tier customer segmentation.
insights.tsTurns all of the above into recommendations + alert seeds, carefully separating observation / cause / recommendation.
industries.tsIndustry packs — the single place a business type is defined (vocabulary, KPIs, labels).
connectors.tsPulls snapshots from external databases/sheets through a shared ingest pipeline.
ingest.tsThe shared path that takes any incoming rows and turns them into a profiled, schema-detected dataset.
crypto.tsAES-GCM encryption for saved connector credentials.
quantiles.tsNumerical primitives (quantiles, distribution math).
report.tsBuilds the structured executive report the PDF exporter renders.
selfcheck.tsA runnable assert-based regression suite for the whole engine — the safety net.
Why this structure is the product’s moat

Because these are pure functions (no database, no network, no clock, no randomness), they are trivially testable and provably repeatable — which is exactly what lets NoPS make its “every number is auditable” promise truthfully. Extending the product means adding one branch to one of these files plus one test. That’s a rare, healthy shape for a codebase this young.

Section 11

The database — what’s stored

PostgreSQL, modelled with Prisma. Every table uses UUID IDs, has created/updated timestamps, is scoped to an organisation, is indexed, and cascades deletes correctly. Here’s the whole data model in plain terms.

TableHolds
UserPeople: email, name, hashed password.
OrganizationA workspace/tenant: name, industry pack, plan tier, plan status, Stripe IDs.
OrganizationMemberLinks a user to an org with a role (Admin / Manager / Viewer).
PasswordResetTokenOne-time tokens for the forgot-password flow.
DatasetAn uploaded/connected dataset: file info, row & column counts, quality score, detected columns, schema map, profiling stats, the original rows, and cleaned rows (JSON).
DataQualityIssueEach detected problem: type, affected rows, severity, recommendation, whether auto-fixable.
ReportA generated report’s structured content.
ForecastA saved forecast: metric, horizon, method, history, and points (with lower/upper bands).
AlertA raised alert: type, severity, metric, value vs threshold, read/unread.
AIConversation / AIMessageChat history. (Historical names — they store user messages and the deterministic engine’s structured responses. No AI-generated numbers are stored.)
ApiKeyThe API-key vault: stored hashed, never returned; only the last four digits kept for display.
ConnectionA saved data-source connector: type, non-secret config, AES-GCM-encrypted secret, last-sync status.
ActivityLogAudit trail of actions per org.
ScheduledReportA report the background scheduler regenerates on a cadence and optionally emails.
AlertRuleA user-defined threshold rule the scheduler evaluates (e.g. “alert if revenue < X, daily”).

Migrations are versioned in apps/api/prisma/migrations/ — you can see the schema evolve: initial → connections → org industry → org plan → scheduling.

One design decision to know

Row data is stored as a JSON blob on the Dataset record, not spread across a per-cell table. That’s simpler and correct for MVP-scale files; the schema comments flag the move to a columnar warehouse if files ever exceed ~100k rows.

Section 14

The big future bet — a generative design system

There is a detailed 470-line architecture document (docs/generative-design-architecture.md) describing where the product wants to go next. It’s the most ambitious idea in the project. Here’s the whole thing in plain terms.

The problem it solves

Today, every business gets the same-shaped dashboard in the same blue — a fixed layout (KPI row → trend → donut → ranking) merely re-labelled per industry. A restaurant, a fintech and a logistics firm all get one skeleton. It’s a strong baseline, but it’s a template, not a design.

The vision

The north star

“NoPS is not a database of dashboards. It is a generative design system capable of composing millions of valid business-intelligence experiences from a constrained set of reusable primitives.” An “AI Design Director” decides what deserves attention and how to present it; deterministic systems still decide what the numbers actually are.

How it would work (without breaking the trust promise)

The key insight is a strict split, enforced by a data contract:

  • The AI proposes presentation only. It emits a DashboardSpec — a strict, versioned, JSON-schema-validated description of which components, layout, style and colours to use. It draws only from a closed vocabulary (it can’t name a component or metric that doesn’t exist).
  • The spec must never contain a number. It carries only references (metricId, seriesId, rankingId). A deterministic renderer resolves those against the real engine outputs at render time. The AI literally cannot fabricate a figure.
  • Two gates. A Spec Validation Gate (is it well-formed?) and a Quality Engine (is the resulting design actually good — hierarchy, readability, accessibility, density?). A design only ships if it clears both.
  • “Generate another design” is first-class. A DesignSeed controls deterministic variation, so the same data can wear “minimal + spacious,” “premium + editorial,” “dense + executive,” or “dark + futuristic” — same numbers, different identity.
  • A Color Intelligence Engine builds accessible, industry-aware palettes (finance navy/gold, food warm terracotta…) — never “a random bright colour per card.”

The rollout plan (phased, additive — not a rewrite)

PhaseWhat ships
AThe DashboardSpec + schema + component registry + a renderer that reproduces today’s dashboard purely from a spec. Proves the boundary. (No AI yet.)
BColor Intelligence + style families (runtime tokens, light/dark, accessibility gates).
CThe AI Design Director emits specs; add DesignSeed + “generate another design.”
DThe Quality Engine + scoring + repair/regenerate loop; v2 becomes default per-dataset with the pack dashboard as fallback.
E(Future) a learning loop that ranks designs from real usage signals.
Reality check

This is designed, not built. It is the single most valuable and most risky piece of future work — valuable because “a dashboard that looks bespoke per business, with numbers you can still audit” is a genuinely differentiated product; risky because the Quality Engine (teaching software to judge “is this design good?”) is hard. The doc is refreshingly honest about the risks: regenerate-loop guards, quality-scoring performance, spec/renderer versioning.

Section 15

Roadmap — what’s next

Pulling together the two roadmap sources (the component roadmap and the generative-design phases) plus the obvious product gaps, here is a sensible ordering by leverage.

Near-term (finish what’s started)

  1. Wire real Stripe billing. The plans, limits and pricing page exist; connecting live checkout turns the app from “usable” to “revenue-capable.” Highest business leverage.
  2. Report builder + templates. Generation, PDF and scheduling are done; a builder and templates make reports self-serve.
  3. Fill in empty/error-state polish and data-prep UI (column mapper, transforms, formula builder) — the rough edges a paying user hits.

Medium-term (the differentiator)

  1. Generative design Phase A–B — the DashboardSpec + renderer + color intelligence. Even without the AI Director, per-industry visual identity would be a visible leap.
  2. Chart interactions — zoom, drill-down, crosshair, export, and geo/choropleth maps.
  3. Collaboration — sharing, comments, activity feed, version history (currently the biggest untouched category).

Longer-term (the vision)

  1. Generative design Phase C–E — the AI Design Director, the Quality Engine gate, and eventually a usage-driven learning loop.
  2. Scale the data layer — move from JSON-on-row storage to a columnar warehouse when file sizes demand it.
  3. More industry packs — each is just one data entry, so breadth is cheap.
The one-line strategic read

The foundation is done and unusually solid. The next dollar is best spent on (a) turning on billing and (b) starting the generative-design layer — the first unlocks revenue, the second unlocks a moat that competitors can’t easily copy without also solving the “trustworthy numbers” problem NoPS already solved.

Section 18

Honest assessment & risks

The part you actually need if you’re deciding what to do next. Straight talk, grounded in what’s in the repo.

Genuine strengths

  • A real product, not a demo. Accounts, teams, permissions, billing tiers, connectors, scheduling — the boring-but-essential scaffolding of a SaaS is present.
  • A defensible, coherent thesis. “Auditable numbers + optional AI convenience” is a real wedge into finance/regulated markets that pure AI tools can’t claim.
  • Unusually clean engineering. Pure engine, strong tests (including money path, tenant isolation, privacy fallback), CI, security hardening, and active removal of dead code.
  • Extensible by design. New industry, insight, chart or intent = one small, tested addition.
  • A clear, ambitious, written-down future that preserves the core promise.

Real risks & gaps

  • No live revenue path yet. Billing is enforced but not charged. Nothing makes money until Stripe is wired.
  • Scale ceiling. JSON-on-row storage is fine now, hard-capped later. Known and documented, but real.
  • The differentiator is unbuilt. The generative-design vision is the moat, and it’s the hardest, least-started work — especially the Quality Engine.
  • No collaboration features. Sharing/comments/versioning is entirely absent — a common expectation for BI tools.
  • Single-author, AI-assisted history. Fast progress, but bus-factor and long-term maintenance are worth planning for.
The verdict in one paragraph

NoPS is a well-engineered, feature-complete MVP with a sharp, defensible identity (trustworthy, deterministic analytics with optional AI convenience) and a genuinely ambitious roadmap (per-business generative design). The foundation is done and clean. The two things standing between it and being a real business are turning on payments and starting to build the visual-design differentiator. For a project barely six weeks old, that is an excellent place to be.
